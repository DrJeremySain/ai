Professor's Lecture Notes: Decomposing Linear Transformations on $\mathbb{R}^2$

1. Direct Term $a \cdot z$

When written in polar form $a = r e^{i\theta}$, multiplying $z$ by $a$ simultaneously scales $z$ by length $r = |a|$ and rotates $z$ counterclockwise by angle $\theta = \text{Arg}(a)$. This represents a spiral similarity that preserves orientation.

2. Conjugate Term $b \cdot \bar{z}$

The complex conjugate $\bar{z} = x - iy$ reflects points across the real axis. Multiplying by $b = s e^{i\phi}$ reflects and rotates $z$. This term introduces orientation reversal, shears, and non-uniform scaling!

3. Matrix Equivalence

Every real $2 \times 2$ affine transformation $\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}A & B \\ C & D\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}c_1 \\ c_2\end{bmatrix}$ is equivalent to $f(z) = az + b\bar{z} + c$ with area scale factor $|\det(M)| = ||a|^2 - |b|^2|$.
