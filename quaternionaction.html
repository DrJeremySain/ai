Mathematical Context: Quaternions as 3D Rotations

The quaternions $\mathbb{H}$ form a 4-dimensional associative division algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ spanned by $\{1, \mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\}$ with fundamental relation $\mathbf{i}^2 = \mathbf{j}^2 = \mathbf{k}^2 = \mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k} = -1$. We identify 3-space $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the subspace of pure imaginary quaternions $\text{Im}(\mathbb{H}) = \{ x_1\mathbf{i} + x_2\mathbf{j} + x_3\mathbf{k} \mid x_i \in \mathbb{R} \}$.

Why Conjugation $x \mapsto a x a^{-1}$?
  • If $x \in \text{Im}(\mathbb{H})$ and $a \in \mathbb{H} \setminus \{0\}$, the conjugated quaternion $x' = a x a^{-1}$ is guaranteed to remain purely imaginary ($x' \in \text{Im}(\mathbb{H})$). Thus, conjugation maps $\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$.
  • Conjugation preserves the quaternion norm: $\|x'\| = \|a x a^{-1}\| = \|a\| \|x\| \|a\|^{-1} = \|x\|$. Therefore, it acts as an isometry (rigid rotation) on $\mathbb{R}^3$.
  • Scaling $a$ by any real factor $c \neq 0$ leaves the action unchanged: $(c a) x (c a)^{-1} = c a x c^{-1} a^{-1} = a x a^{-1}$. Hence, every non-zero quaternion acts identically to its unit counterpart $\hat{a} = a/\|a\|$.
Connection to Euler's Axis-Angle Parameterization
  • Any unit quaternion can be written as $\hat{a} = \cos(\theta/2) + \mathbf{u} \sin(\theta/2)$, where $\mathbf{u} = u_1\mathbf{i} + u_2\mathbf{j} + u_3\mathbf{k}$ is a unit vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
  • The transformation $x \mapsto \hat{a} x \hat{a}^{-1}$ corresponds to a spatial rotation of the vector $x$ about the axis $\mathbf{u}$ by an angle of $\theta$ (counter-clockwise by right-hand rule).
  • Notice the double cover phenomenon: $\hat{a}$ and $-\hat{a}$ produce the exact same rotation on $\mathbb{R}^3$, giving the 2-to-1 group homomorphism $Sp(1) \to SO(3)$.
